1. INTRODUCTION
Flexible and wearable electronics require conductors that can bend, stretch, and retain
conductivity, a requirement that rigid metal wires and foils cannot fulfill effectively
when integrated into biological interfaces. Conducting fibers, fabricated via routes
such as solution spinning, coating, and electrospinning, are being rapidly developed to
meet this need. In biomedical applications, particularly peripheral nerve regeneration,
the specific challenge extends beyond basic conductivity to encompass fiber alignment.
Moving from randomly oriented electrospun fiber mats to uniaxially aligned arrays provides directional conductivity and topographical cues that guide Schwann cell migration
and axonal outgrowth. This report documents the experimental work completed thus far
on the electrospinning of polycaprolactone (PCL) solutions to produce randomly oriented nanofibers, alongside the refined methodology for upcoming polymer blend trials
(PLA/PCL) and solvent-variation trials using Trifluoroethanol (TFE) and Acetone mixtures
